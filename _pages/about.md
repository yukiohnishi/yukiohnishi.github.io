I am an incoming assistant professor in the Department of Statistics at the University of Florida. Prior to joining UF, I was a postdoctoral associate at the Yale School of Public Health (YSPH), where I worked with Professor Fan Li. I received my Ph.D. in Statistics from Purdue University, where I was co-advised by Professors Jordan Awan and Arman Sabbaghi.


My primary research centers on developing theory and methods for causal inference under complex data structures, with an emphasis on Bayesian methodology. I am particularly interested in post-treatment confounding, mediation analysis, interference and spillovers, sequential treatments, time-to-event outcomes, and complex randomized experiments, including cluster-randomized and two-stage trials. I also work on differential privacy, particularly at its interface with causal inference and on privacy–utility trade-offs in statistical modeling.
